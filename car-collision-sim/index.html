Crossing the Stream

The amber box on the road is the danger window drawn on the ground. If a blue car's nose is inside it at the moment the yellow car leaves the start line, they will collide. Watch for the amber dot on a blue car's nose: that is a crash waiting to happen. At launch the page states what the math predicts, then the cars play it out.

Why it is 3/11

Ride along with the stream so its cars stand still. The crossing car then moves diagonally backwards through the parked line. A crash happens if its footprint touches a stream car anywhere on that path.

Line up the positions where a stream car could be when the crossing starts and still get hit. They form one unbroken window made of four lengths:

Stream car length Lhits full body has to pass the crossing car1 m
Crossing car width wvthe stream car must clear its full width0.5 m
Crossing car length × vh/vvhow far the stream moves while the crossing car's body passes1 × 1 = 1 m
Stream car width × vh/vvhow far the stream moves while the crossing car traverses the lane0.5 × 1 = 0.5 m
Danger window3 m
One car plus one gap (repeat length)11 m

The random start time makes the stream's position uniform over one repeat length, so the chance of a crash is the window divided by the repeat length: 3 / 11 = 27.27%.

P = [ Lh + wv + (vh/vv)(Lv + wh) ] / (Lh + gap)

Assumes the stream cars are also ½ m wide. If the 10 m in the sketch means centre to centre instead of the gap, the repeat length is 10 m and the answer is 3/10 = 30%. Use the preset below to see that case.

Monte Carlo check

Each trial picks a uniformly random start time, then steps both cars through the crossing in 0.8 ms increments and tests whether their rectangles overlap. The formula is not used.
